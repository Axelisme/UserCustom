# Migrate to Orchestrate v159

## S3's counter becomes an instrument

`Machine rework: N/2` was a budget: the third production correction was a scope event, and the two
routes out of it were recutting at S1 or reporting. It is now `Machine rework: N`, monotonic and
unbounded. Two is the advisory point where the count starts being worth explaining; no count blocks
a dispatch, invalidates a Slice, or requires re-admission.

The counter was thresholding the wrong thing, in the same way S1's production-diff limit did before
v155 retired it. What predicts an unrecoverable lane is not how many corrections it took but whether
they kept superseding each other — S2.8 already says this about Contracts ("a correction is not
self-validating, and one has already introduced the defect that superseded it") while S3 counted.
Rather than build the cause-tracking that would threshold the right variable, S3 stops thresholding
and records instead.

So from the third increment on, Root adds one line naming what the preceding corrections' diagnosis
missed, and the count and those lines travel into the Slice's acceptance receipt. Four independent
defects found by successively deeper gates and one misdiagnosis corrected four times leave the same
number behind and want opposite handling; the line is what separates them, written where it is
cheapest to write rather than reconstructed at review.

## Recut is removed

Recut was already one of two options at the scope event ("Recut at S1 or report it"). With the scope
event gone it has no trigger, and it is removed rather than left as a route an agent may take on its
own. When a Slice needs recutting the user directs it; S1 admission of the replacement is unchanged.

Deleted with it: the paragraph stating that recutting resets the counter, and the hole that paragraph
documented — a need re-cut five times spent five admission and dispatch cycles that no check fired
on. A counter that never resets cannot be escaped by recutting, so both go together.

S2.8's second-failure clause loses its `recut` branch and keeps reporting. S1.1's "the rework budget
is half spent" loses the word budget, which stopped being accurate.

## What this costs

Nothing bounds a Slice's correction loop mechanically any more. The failure mode moves from being
interrupted too early to grinding a bad diagnosis without anyone calling stop, and it is now visible
only afterwards, in the acceptance receipt. That trade is deliberate. On the task that produced this
change one Slice spent its full budget and recut, and a recut ticket there re-created by hand the
predecessor tests that had never been in question: the bound cost work without bounding the thing
that had actually gone wrong.

## Preserved mechanisms

Text only. No CLI grammar, payload key, ref, trailer, exit code, or lifecycle step changes. Nothing
reads the counter line; it is prose Root writes and a reader reads. Historical tickets recording
`Machine rework: N/2` stay as they are — they record what was true under the version that admitted
them.
